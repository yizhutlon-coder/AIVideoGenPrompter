# PromptStudio output audit — 2026-09-17

Scope: the twelve prompt translators in `PromptStudio.html`, using the locally installed `qwen2.5:7b-instruct` through Ollama at temperature 0.7. These are prompt-output checks; no image or video was rendered by the target generation models.

Inputs used:

- Video: “In a dusty theater, a ballerina steps forward, spins, then bows. The camera slowly pushes in. No dialogue; soft footsteps and room ambience.”
- Image: “A woman in a red coat holds a ceramic cup beside a rainy cafe window. Her left hand rests on the table; soft side light.”
- H3 multi-ref: the video request plus `refs: picture 1 = photograph of a ballerina in a blue dress, video 1 = clip showing a slow camera push-in`.
- Cross-mode check: the video request was also sent to image targets to see whether they mistakenly describe audio or camera movement in a still image.

The strongest remaining limit is the text-only local 7B writer. It can ignore a negative instruction, borrow details from a worked example, or add plausible but unrequested objects. The format checker can identify concrete contradictions but cannot prove that every generated visual detail was supplied by the user. “Format OK” means the structural checks passed, not that the prompt is factually faithful or that the target model will render it correctly.

H3 regression input: `picture 1= the character reference. picture 2= supplies the scene, and the outfit. the woman in picture 2 serves as a position guide, and is replaced by the woman in picture 1. the woman from image 1 jumps around the battle field depicted in picture 2 cutting down enemies.` Before the fix, the local model invented `<Video 1>` and weaponry. After tightening the instructions, a local run still defined `<Subject 2>`; its repair invented `<Video 2>`. Both are now caught. The deterministic fallback for this exact structure includes only the two listed pictures and one final subject. This validates label and role containment, not visual fidelity to images the translator cannot see.

The format instructions themselves also created pressure to fill gaps: they asked for style sentences, camera amplitude and speed, a 350–500-word description, soundscape prose, and a worked example whose output added facts absent from its input. Those requirements were removed or made conditional on user text. The replacement worked example has four assets and two shots, with every output fact explicitly present in its example input. A brief H3 request now offers the user a choice to add detail or continue with a short, faithful version. A local repeat after these changes no longer added a video, weapon, or sound, but still defined the discarded guide as `<Subject 2>`; the role guard catches that and routes this pattern to the text-derived fallback.

## Grounding pass after the single-image battlefield example

User's short request: “the woman from image 1 jumps around the battlefield cutting down enemies.” The original H3 translation added armor, sword, debris, multiple shots, camera moves, grunts, explosions, and orchestral music. A deterministic check against the pasted output now flags eleven categories of unsupported content. The three H3 field names and `[Shot 1]` can be retained without adding scene facts; absent sound and music are `N/A`.

The same request was sent once to each other target through locally installed `qwen2.5:7b-instruct` at temperature 0.2 after tightening its format instructions. Wan T2V, Wan I2V, LTX, SDXL photoreal, Flux, and Z-Image stayed close to the source. H3 still added a camera; SCAIL-2 and SDXL anime added a sword; Qwen-Image changed the positive prompt to Chinese and added a sword; Krea 2 added battle attire, a sword, spinning, and visual effects. The shared grounding check now catches these observed additions, retries once, and otherwise uses a direct text-derived simple draft when that target supports one. This is a smoke test of one request per target, not proof of complete semantic grounding.

Every translator now offers “Add details” or “Use simple version” for brief requests. Common instructions forbid adding scene facts, model examples with fabricated source details were removed or rewritten, and the initial local writer uses temperature 0.2. Length-floor warnings that pushed the Fix action toward padding were removed. Copying, pinning, revisions, and old history now use the originating request for grounding checks; a successful refinement records its added request details for that target. Project context and selected Chinese output are captured as allowed source information. Existing pinned entries without an originating request cannot be verified and are not copied.

Regression checks: the user's original three-field H3 output triggers eleven unsupported-content categories. The simple draft for the battlefield sentence passes full format checks for all 11 non-reference targets; the two-picture H3 multi-reference draft passes its full-reference checks. A separate picture-plus-video reference request also passes the generic simple draft. A detailed H3 request containing its own red coat, blue light, sword, camera pan, cut time, and orchestral music passes the grounding checks without a false flag. The checks are deliberately conservative and cannot establish that every noun in arbitrary prose came from the request; choosing the direct simple draft gives the strongest text-only guarantee.

## Follow-up: false-positive and end-to-end testing

The first pushback rule used raw word count. It interrupted concise but complete requests (for example, a 25-word scene with colors, camera, weather, and sound). It now considers the kinds of scene details supplied. A short battlefield sentence and a role-only H3 reference request still offer the choice; concise requests with explicit visual, camera, or sound details translate directly. The message now says details are unstated rather than asserting that the input is short.

The first grounding check incorrectly rejected ordinary variants: `smiling`/`smiles`, `gray`/`grey`, plural `shots` and `cuts`, and an unquoted line supplied by the user. Those cases now pass. It now also catches tested additions it previously missed: a castle, hairstyle, extra wave, and a still picture falsely cited as the source of motion. This is a list of observed cases, not an exhaustive semantic verifier.

Manual review of the first fully passing sparse run found two more misses despite its clean automated result: H3 added “combat gear,” and the anime prompt contained empty category tags (`hair, eyes, clothing, pose, ...`) plus `negative: N/A`. The checker now rejects those exact patterns. Its broad category check was then narrowed to image targets because `outfit` is a valid asset role in H3 reference prompts; this removed a false positive on the simple H3 reference draft. A repeat sparse run passed all 12 targets, and its output was read again for unsupported scene words.

An end-to-end run of the actual Translate path found outputs that passed the grounding check but failed a target format rule: lost `then` sequencing, audible content in Wan I2V, invalid Pony quality tags, and an inactive negative prompt on Z-Image Turbo. Generation now repairs the complete set of hard validation issues before displaying an output, then uses a checked direct draft when possible; otherwise it withholds the invalid result. The same full check gates copying, pinning, and revisions. A stalled local response is bounded to 120 seconds and 1,400 generated tokens.

The H3 reference fallback also dropped a sound explicitly supplied by the user. It now carries stated sound into `overall_soundscape` and keeps stated character details in the subject definition. With project context enabled, “Use simple version” previously skipped the direct draft and called the model; it now builds a direct draft from both user-provided sources.

The repeatable harness is `research/_addenda/promptstudio-grounding-harness.mjs`. Its deterministic cases cover the detail-choice decision, accepted wording variants, concrete inventions, copy, pin, Fix, and per-target refinement provenance. The final run completed **48 of 48** target-path checks: the local Qwen 7B writer on a detailed and a sparse request across all 12 targets, and direct simple drafts with and without project context across all 12. The saved output is `research/_addenda/promptstudio-grounding-live-results-2026-09-17.json`. The older 142-fixture validator set contains “gold” outputs that intentionally embellish minimal inputs; those labels predate the user's strict no-invention rule and are not suitable as grounding success criteria.

Remaining limit: the checker recognizes common classes of invented detail and target-format defects. It cannot prove that arbitrary prose has no new facts or that every fact from a complex request survived. A user who needs exact text-only grounding should choose the direct simple draft and review the final prompt.
