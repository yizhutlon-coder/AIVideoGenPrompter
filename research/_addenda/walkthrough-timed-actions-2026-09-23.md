# Walk me through it: timed H3 actions and subjects — 2026-09-23

The guided H3 form now lets users list on-screen subjects separately from reference assets. A subject can be identified from a numbered picture or solely by a description. An added subject requires an identifying cue; when there are several subjects, Subject 1 needs a cue too. Scene, outfit, and other reference roles remain separate labels, so a scene picture does not silently become the second person.

Opening actions and timed actions are separate. Each timed row accepts a time in seconds, actor, action, optional target subject, exact dialogue, language, and an explicit new-shot checkbox. A six-second row becomes `At 00:06.000` inside Shot 1 unless the user chooses a cut; a chosen cut produces the next `[Shot N]` marker. A combined action and line uses `while saying` and preserves the entered words inside `<d>[Language] ...</d>`. The guide does not infer a camera move, violence detail, or sound effect from the action. A clip duration from 5 to 15 seconds is carried into H3 Builder on handoff.

The user's example was tested with two women identified from Picture 1 and a battlefield from Picture 2. At six seconds, Subject 2 runs toward screen right, punches Subject 1, and says exactly “This is for what you did.” The result kept all three reference roles distinct and did not add a shot cut. The live handoff showed both references, the timed line, and a ten-second duration in H3 Builder.

The original timed-actions pass covered 22 cases, including dialogue-only beats, explicit cuts, invalid end times, backwards timing, self-targeting, ambiguous subjects, and a subject without a separate picture. The existing target validator accepts the valid prompts. These checks establish prompt structure and browser behavior; they do not measure how accurately H3 will realize exact timing, motion, or dialogue in a rendered video.

## Custom actors and H3 rule check

The actor choices now include tagged subjects, **Everyone** (all listed subjects), and **Custom actor** with a free-text field. Timed actions also accept a custom target. Use an on-screen subject row for a recurring person or group from a reference asset; custom text is useful for a one-off actor description. Typing “everyone” into Custom also expands to the listed subjects. Everyone with a spoken line uses a compound ID such as `(S1,S2)`, and speaker IDs are allocated by first vocal event rather than by `<Subject N>` number. Exact dialogue appears once in `detailed_description`, not in `summary`. If ambient or physical sounds are not supplied, the soundscape says `Unspecified.` rather than implying complete silence with `N/A`.

These choices follow MiniMax's [base video prompt guide](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3/blob/main/docs/VIDEO_PROMPT_WRITING_GUIDE_base_en.md) for group speech and first-vocal-event speaker IDs, and its [full-reference guide](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3/blob/main/docs/VIDEO_PROMPT_WRITING_GUIDE_ref_en.md) for stable `<Subject N>` labels and dialogue placement. The harness now passes 29 cases, including everyone acting, group dialogue, a custom actor/target, speaker order, and missing custom text. Browser checks confirmed the custom fields appear when selected and a two-subject group line assembled with `(S1,S2)`.

The browser handoff to H3 Builder preserved the two subject definitions, the combined speaker ID, exact dialogue, two reference pictures, and eight-second duration. H3 Builder reported only its expected advisory that this intentionally sparse description is below its normal detail recommendation. The walkthrough harness passed 29/29, the parent grounding harness passed 26/26, JavaScript parsed, the diff had no whitespace errors, and the release checksum check passed.

## Optional local AI review

The guide now offers **Check with selected AI** after it assembles and rule-checks a draft inside Prompt Studio. The selected installed Ollama model receives the filled-field summary and assembled prompt, then gives an advisory comparison. The original draft is not edited or replaced. The review is shown as text, with the model name, and is discarded when the form changes or a new draft is assembled. Standalone `WalkThrough.html` continues to assemble prompts without a model; the AI button appears when hosted by the main app.

The AI's judgment is still advisory and cannot inspect the actual media or predict model output.
